Getting Comfortable in Claude

A self-paced tour of the Claude interface. Work through the stations in order the first time, then come back to any single one later as a quick reference.

How to use this lab: Each station opens to a short explanation and a prompt you can paste directly into Claude to see the feature in action. Click a station to expand it. 2 The navigation sidebarFind your way around the left-hand menu ▾

Everything you'll use lives on the left. Take a moment to notice each piece:

  • New chat — starts a fresh conversation. Every question begins here.
  • Chat history — the running list of your past conversations, newest at the top. Click any one to reopen it exactly where you left off.
  • Projects — folders that keep related chats and files together (its own station below).
  • Search — a magnifying glass to find something you discussed before (also its own station).
  • Your name / settings gear — bottom of the sidebar. This is where account settings, memory, and preferences live.

If the sidebar is collapsed, look for the small panel icon in the top-left corner to open it back up.

3 Memory & settingsWhat Claude remembers, and how to control it ▾

Open Settings from the gear or your name at the bottom of the sidebar. A few things are worth knowing here.

Memory lets Claude carry helpful details from one conversation into the next — things like your role, the courses you teach, or how you like responses formatted — so you don't have to reintroduce yourself each time. You can turn it on or off, and you can review or delete what it has stored. If you'd rather keep every chat completely separate, leave memory off.

Also worth a look while you're in settings: your display and appearance options, and where to manage your account. You don't need to change anything today — the goal is just to know where these controls are.

4 Starting your first chatThe mechanics of sending a message ▾

Click New chat, type into the message box at the bottom, and press Enter (or the send arrow) to send. Claude replies just below your message, and the conversation keeps scrolling as you go.

  • Press Shift + Enter to add a line break without sending — useful for longer messages.
  • You can keep the back-and-forth going; Claude remembers everything said within that conversation.
  • Start a New chat whenever you switch to an unrelated topic — it keeps things tidy and focused.

That's all there is to the mechanics. (How to phrase a great request is covered in the prompting lab.)

Try it in Claude

Hi Claude — this is my first time using you. Introduce yourself in a couple of sentences and tell me one useful thing you can help a faculty member with.

5 The plus (+) menuAdd files and more to a message ▾

Next to the message box you'll see a + button. This is how you bring things into a conversation rather than just typing.

  • Upload a file — a PDF, Word document, spreadsheet, slide deck, or image. Claude can then read, summarize, or work from it.
  • Add a photo or screenshot — handy for asking about something you can see but can't easily type out.
  • Depending on your setup, you may also see options to connect other tools here.

Once a file is attached, just type your request about it in the same message — for example, "summarize the key points of this."

6 Changing models & effortPick how Claude thinks about a task ▾

Near the top of the chat (and by the message box) you'll find a small menu showing the current model. Claude comes in a few models that trade speed for depth:

  • Lighter, faster models are great for quick questions and everyday tasks.
  • More capable models take a little longer but handle complex reasoning, nuanced writing, and harder problems better.

Some models also let you dial up the effort or "thinking" — asking Claude to reason more carefully before answering. Use more effort for tricky or high-stakes work, and keep it light for simple things. You can switch anytime; there's no wrong choice while you're exploring.

7 Voice modeTalk to Claude instead of typing ▾

Look for a microphone or voice icon near the message box. Voice mode lets you speak your request out loud instead of typing it — useful when you're thinking out loud, multitasking, or just prefer talking.

  • Tap the voice icon and allow microphone access the first time.
  • Speak naturally; Claude transcribes what you say and responds.
  • You can switch back to typing at any point in the same conversation.

Availability can vary by device and app version, so if you don't see it, it may not be enabled in your current setup.

8 ProjectsKeep related chats and files together ▾

A Project is a workspace that groups related conversations and reference files in one place. Think of it like a folder for a single course, committee, or ongoing effort.

  • Create one from Projects in the sidebar, give it a name, and start chatting inside it.
  • Add reference files or standing instructions once, and every chat in that project can draw on them — no need to re-upload each time.
  • Great for anything you return to repeatedly, like a course you teach each term.

Chats you start inside a project stay grouped there, separate from your general chat history.

9 ArtifactsWhen Claude builds something you can open and edit ▾

When you ask Claude to create something substantial — a document, a handout, a table, a simple web page — it often opens in a panel beside the chat called an Artifact. This keeps the finished piece separate from the conversation so you can read and work with it comfortably.

  • The artifact appears to the right; your chat stays on the left.
  • Ask for changes in the chat and the artifact updates in place.
  • You can usually copy or download it to use elsewhere.

You don't have to do anything special to trigger one — just ask Claude to write or build something, and an artifact appears when it makes sense.

10 Search past chatsFind something you discussed before ▾

Over time your chat history grows, and scrolling isn't always the fastest way back to something. Use Search (the magnifying glass in the sidebar) to jump straight to a past conversation.

  • Type a word or phrase you remember using, and matching chats appear.
  • Click a result to reopen that conversation right where it was.
  • Helpful when you know you worked something out before but can't remember which chat it was in.

12 CoworkWhen you'd reach for it instead of a regular chatStretch ▾

A regular chat is perfect for questions, drafting, and back-and-forth. Cowork is a different mode for when you want Claude to actually work with the files on your computer — reading a folder of documents, organizing files, or producing finished files you can open directly.

The simplest way to think about it: reach for a chat when you want to talk something through, and reach for Cowork when you want hands-on help with real files and multi-step tasks. You don't need to master this today — just know it exists and what it's for.
